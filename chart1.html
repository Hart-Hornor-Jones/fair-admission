An imperfect predictor

Grades, scores, outcomeshow loosely an admissions indicator tracks what happens later

Admissions offices rank applicants on indicators they can see in the fall of senior year: high school grades, a test score, or some blend. What they are trying to predict happens later. Here is a simulated pool of 30,000 applicants to a university like UC. Each dot is one student. Across: the indicator admissions offices have found most informative, high school GPA and SAT combined. Up: the grades that student would earn in the first year of college if admitted. The pool is built from what UC has published about its own applicants and students: the spread of grades and scores in each group, and how first-year grades follow from them.

How the pool was simulated, and what UC reported

Applicants. Four groups in the shares implied by the STTF report's Table 3C-2 for fall 2018 UC applicants (Latino 39%, Asian 32%, White 23%, African American 7%, after dropping other and unknown). Within each group, weighted-capped high school GPA and SAT (reading + math) are drawn from normal distributions with the means and standard deviations printed on the STTF report's Figures 3C-4 and 3C-10 (Appendix V, Table 1), fall 2018 applicants:

HSGPA is capped at 4.4 and SAT at 1600. Within each group HSGPA and SAT correlate 0.45, which gives a pooled correlation near the 0.53 (R² = 28%) the STTF reports among applicants.

First-year grades. Each student's first-year UC GPA is built from the regression UC's Institutional Research office fit to the 2015 entering cohort (IRAP 2020, Appendix C): standardized coefficients 0.23 on HSGPA and 0.36 on SAT, 25% of variance explained. Those are converted to raw units with enrollee standard deviations of 0.31 (HSGPA), 155 (SAT) and 0.56 (first-year GPA, backed out of the 2003–06 UCOP regression tables), so one HSGPA point is worth about 0.42 grade points and 100 SAT points about 0.13. Each group then gets the small offset from the common line that Geiser & Studley (2001, Table 8) measured for the HSGPA + SAT I model (last column above), plus normal noise with SD 0.485. The intercept is set so that the 60% of applicants admitted on a holistic reading have a mean first-year GPA of 3.05; grades are capped at 4.0. Nothing is fit to the outcome correlations directly — they are what the model produces:

The "top 60%" column keeps the 60% of applicants highest on a holistic reading that correlates 0.7 with the combined indicator, standing in for everything else in a file: UC can only observe the students it enrolls, and a relationship measured in that narrowed pool reads weaker than in the applicant pool, so this is the column to compare with UC's reported figures. The combined indicator is the regression's own weighting of HSGPA and SAT, shown as a standard score with mean 50 and SD 10 over the pool. Nothing here is UC student-level data.
